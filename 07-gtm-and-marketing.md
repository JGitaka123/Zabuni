# 07 — Go-to-Market

## The name

**Zabuni** — Swahili for *tender / bid / quotation*.

Why it works: instantly legible to every procurement and sales person in Kenya, Tanzania and Uganda without explanation; describes the product's front door; ranks naturally alongside high-intent local search vocabulary; pronounceable and spellable over a phone call, which matters more than it sounds when your sales motion involves people reading a URL aloud.

Why it's a risk: it is a common noun, so trademark protection will be narrow and you must build the mark through use and design rather than relying on registration. It also anchors on quoting, when the product's centre of gravity moves to collections. Mitigate with the tagline.

**Tagline: "From RFQ to paid."** — states the full loop, and pre-empts the "isn't this just quoting software" objection.

**Alternates**, if `zabuni.co.ke` / `.com` are unavailable or trademark counsel objects:
- **Tarifa** — Swahili *report/notice*, and cognate with *tariff*. Broader, more abstract, more defensible as a mark, less immediately legible.
- **Mzigo** — Swahili *consignment/load*. Very memorable and distinctly East African, but reads as logistics rather than finance.

**Do first, before any design work:** check `zabuni.co.ke`, `zabuni.com`, `zabuni.africa`; search the KIPI trademark register in classes 9, 35 and 42; check the Business Registration Service for name conflicts; secure matching handles.

## Positioning

**To a distributor:**
> Zabuni turns RFQs into eTIMS-compliant quotes in minutes, then chases the money until it lands.

**To the market (the ad-level claim):**
> Your customers can't claim an expense without a valid eTIMS invoice. Zabuni makes every invoice compliant automatically — and collects it 20 days faster.

Note what leads. Compliance is the door because it is legally forced, deadline-shaped and currently painful. Collections is the value because it is money. Speed is the proof. AI is never the headline — in this market it lowers trust rather than raising it, and "AI-powered" reads as unproven to a 55-year-old distributor with KES 4M outstanding.

## Sequencing

**Phase 0 — Safuney (months 0–3).** Build it for one real distributor. Instrument everything. Produce the two numbers that will carry the entire go-to-market: RFQ-to-quote time before and after, and DSO before and after. Do not sell to anyone else until these exist.

**Phase 1 — Three design partners (months 3–6).** Paid, not free — a free pilot teaches you nothing about willingness to pay and produces customers who won't complain honestly. Pick from adjacent categories: one medical/lab consumables, one agro-inputs, one industrial chemicals. Founder-led sales, warm intros only.

**Phase 2 — Paid acquisition (months 6+).** Only after the funnel converts warm leads reliably. Paid traffic into a funnel that doesn't convert just buys you expensive proof that it doesn't convert.

## Google Ads — the primary channel

This is where the compliance wedge pays for itself, because it converts *existing* high-intent demand rather than manufacturing it.

**Tier 1, high intent, buy immediately:**
`eTIMS integration software` · `eTIMS API Kenya` · `eTIMS compliant invoicing` · `eTIMS ERP integration` · `KRA eTIMS invoice software` · `eTIMS for distributors`

Low volume, extremely high intent, cheap relative to value. Someone searching this has a deadline and a problem.

**Tier 2, adjacent:**
`quotation software Kenya` · `invoicing software Kenya` · `M-Pesa invoice reconciliation` · `debtor management software Kenya` · `accounts receivable software Kenya`

**Tier 3, competitor and category:** ERP vendor names, generic accounting software terms. Lower priority, higher cost.

**Landing pages, one per keyword cluster**, each with a single job. The eTIMS page must show a real eTIMS invoice with a visible CUIN and scannable QR code. The market has been burned by vendors whose "eTIMS support" was an unreliable bolt-on; showing the artefact is the whole argument, and the standing advice circulating among Kenyan buyers is literally to demand a vendor demonstrate a live eTIMS invoice before purchase. Meet that demand on the landing page.

## Facebook / Instagram

Different job: Google captures demand, Meta creates it and retargets.

**Targeting:** Kenya, business owners and finance/procurement job titles, interests in business software and wholesale/distribution. Lookalikes from the customer list once you have 100+ (not before — small seed lists produce noise).

**Creative angles, ranked:**
1. **The penalty** — "KES 1 million penalty. 10% of tax. Expenses disallowed." Fear-led, but factually accurate. Works because it is true and imminent.
2. **The DSO number** — "KES 3.2M outstanding. 67 days average. Here's what changed." Concrete, from the Safuney case study.
3. **The speed demo** — screen recording: photo of a printed RFQ in, finished quote out, timer visible. This is the single most convincing 20 seconds you can produce, and it needs no narration.
4. **Founder-to-owner** — direct address from a distributor who built this for his own business. Credibility in this market is personal, not corporate. Use it.

**Click-to-WhatsApp ads.** Route Meta traffic straight into a WhatsApp conversation rather than a web form. Kenyan SMEs abandon forms and answer WhatsApp. The first-touch agent qualifies (turnover, RFQ volume, VAT status, current eTIMS state) and books a demo. This also makes your own product the first thing a prospect experiences — dogfooding as demo.

**Budget discipline:** start at KES 60,000/month across both channels. Do not scale spend until CAC is under KES 90,000 and a cohort has renewed. Applying the same sample-size guardrail from `docs/04`: do not draw conclusions from a creative with 40 clicks.

## Other channels

- **KAM, KEPSA, and sector associations** — distributor trade bodies are where this ICP already gathers. Sponsor an eTIMS compliance workshop; it is a sales pitch that people thank you for.
- **Accountants and tax consultants as referrers.** They feel the eTIMS pain most acutely on their clients' behalf and they have the trust. Build a referral commission. This may become the best channel you have.
- **Content/SEO** — an eTIMS compliance guide that is genuinely better than what currently ranks. There is a real gap; most existing content is vendor-thin.
- **GitakaLab** — health-sector credibility and doors into medical/lab distribution. Adjacent, not integrated. Use for introductions, keep the businesses separate.

## Onboarding funnel

The conversion killer is catalog import and tax classification. A distributor with 800 SKUs in a messy Excel file will not do this themselves, and if you ask them to, they will stall and churn before ever seeing value.

So: **do it for them, and charge for it.** The onboarding fee funds a human plus LLM-assisted classification. Target time-to-first-eTIMS-invoice under 5 business days, and time-to-first-agent-recovered-payment under 21 days. Track both as your leading indicators — they predict retention better than any usage metric.

## Sales assets

1. **The Safuney case study** — before/after on quote latency and DSO, with the holdout comparison. Your most valuable asset. Build it properly.
2. **The live eTIMS demo** — a real invoice, real CUIN, scannable QR.
3. **The 20-second speed video.**
4. **A DSO calculator** — lead magnet. Owner enters receivables and average days; it returns cash locked up and what a 20-day improvement is worth. Gated on phone number, routes into WhatsApp.
